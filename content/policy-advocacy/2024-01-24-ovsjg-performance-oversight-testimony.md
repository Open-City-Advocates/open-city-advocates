**Performance Oversight Hearing**

**Office of Victim Services and Justice Grants (OVSJG)**

**January 24, 2024**

**Committee on the Judiciary and Public Safety**

**Ward 2 Councilmember Brooke Pinto, Chairperson**

## Testimony of Penelope Spain

### Chief Executive Officer, Open City Advocates

Good afternoon. My name is Joshua Miller and I am the Research & Advocacy Director for Open City Advocates and a Ward 4 resident. Due to the death of a family member yesterday, our CEO, Penelope Spain, is unable to be here. I'll be delivering Open City Advocates' testimony on her behalf. We provide legal representation and holistic advocacy to youth who have been committed to the DC Department of Youth Rehabilitation Services (DYRS), including throughout their reentry from secure facilities.

Good afternoon, Councilmembers. My name is Penelope Spain. I am a resident of Ward 4 and for the past 19 years have served as CEO of Open City Advocates, which provides legal representation and holistic advocacy to youth who have been committed to the DC Department of Youth Rehabilitation Services (DYRS), including throughout their reentry from secure facilities. If our clients find themselves in the adult criminal legal system, we continue supporting them throughout their subsequent supervision, incarceration, and reentry. Open City Advocates also engages in systemic reform efforts and is a member of numerous coalitions and working groups dedicated to supporting returning citizens, including the DC Reentry Action Network (RAN), which OVSJG helped launch a few years ago.

I'd like to start today by expressing our profound gratitude to the Office of Victim Services and Justice Grants for their steadfast support of Open City Advocates. In 2006, only one year after establishing our organization, OVSJG saw our potential and provided critical seed funding. At the time, OCA was still operating out of a closet, without heat or air conditioning, in a warehouse next to Marvin Gaye Park in Ward 7. Eighteen years later, Open City Advocates has assisted over 165 court-involved youth and, through impact litigation, successfully established the right to legal counsel for all children throughout their commitment to DYRS.

OVSJG's steadfast support has allowed us to uplift the lives of youth returning from both juvenile and adult incarceration, ensuring that they have a voice in the services they receive. By advocating for carefully tailored reentry plans and through intensive community support, we have been able to cut recidivism by half (compared to those who don't receive intensive post-commitment representation), thereby preventing future incarceration and improving public safety.

OVSJG's financial support is critical to the smooth and consistent operation of our program. The support of its staff, however, goes beyond the financial management of its grants. Our OVSJG Program Officer often brainstorms with us about new partnerships and provides ongoing feedback. In addition, we regularly receive support from our Program Officer on case-specific issues. For example, if we're struggling to find safe housing for a client, we can turn to our OVSJG contact to help make connections to government agencies or other grantees to meet the need. As a recipient of dozens of grants each year from public and private sources, I can attest that the programmatic support we receive from OVSJG is second to none. OVSJG's ability to provide strong financial oversight alongside programmatic support makes it a standout government office striking the perfect balance of efficiency and humanity.

Over the years, the support we've received from OVSJG has enabled Open City Advocates to expand our services to reach more youth and make our city safer. Through the federal ARPA flex funds, we're able to provide direct assistance to our clients by providing them education and workforce development stipends, as well as transportation, groceries, and cell phone service, all of which are critical to supporting their education and employment goals. Lastly, OVSJG funding allows us to pay stipends to youth engaged in leadership development opportunities through the DC Juvenile Justice Advisory Group. Watching our young, homegrown youth leaders develop into the next generation of youth advocates and social justice reformers is one of the most fulfilling parts of our work. OVSJG makes that possible.

The funds we receive from OVSJG support DC residents with the highest needs. Our clients struggle regularly with obtaining stable housing, vital documents, adequate mental health support, substance use and recovery support, and meaningful education and employment opportunities. Over the past few years, with ARPA funds provided by OVSJG, we have been able to make a huge impact in the lives of our clients who are returning from incarceration by providing substantial and meaningful financial support. This has been in the form of stipends for going to school or attending vocational programs, groceries when public benefits have not been enough, clothing, furniture, and even cell phones, laptops, and internet service, which are critical for educational and employment success. Even such simple and cost-effective solutions make a huge difference for our clients' stability. OVSJG provides the robust funding for both juvenile and adult reentry support that engenders new and innovative solutions while also providing the critical continuity of support that transforms service providers like Open City Advocates into leading models of reentry support in the District of Columbia.

### Conclusion

On behalf of Open City Advocates, I would like to extend my gratitude to OVSJG for its continued support of and partnership in our work. Robust funding and programmatic support for juvenile and adult reentry services is exactly what the District needs right now. Thank you for your time and consideration.
