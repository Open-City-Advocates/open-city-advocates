**Statement of Open City Advocates**  
**Before the Committee on the Judiciary & Public Safety, Council of the District of Columbia**  
**Public Hearing on Bill 24-0338, the "Redefinition of Child Amendment Act of 2021"**

**October 7, 2021**

## Testimony of Penelope Spain, Chief Executive Officer

### Oral Testimony

Good morning, Chairman Allen, Councilmember Pinto, and Councilmember Mc Duffie,

My name is Penelope Spain and I am the CEO and Co-Founder of Open City Advocates, which provides legal representation and holistic mentoring to youth who have been committed to the DC Department of Youth Rehabilitation Services. Our target population is teens 16 years of age or older who are most at-risk of entering the adult criminal legal system.

Open City Advocates resolutely supports the Redefinition of the Child Amendment Act of 2021. During my 17 years serving justice-involved youth, I have seen too many promising lives swept into the adult legal system through our jurisdiction’s direct file laws. With no judicial oversight and seemingly arbitrary decisions by the US Attorney’s Office, teens who have already suffered so much trauma are tossed into a system that denies their humanity and further scars them, both physically and emotionally, at the very moment that they are trying to gain their footing as young adults.

As you may remember, I have come before you and the DC Council many times over the past decade to push for improvements to DC’s juvenile legal system and to DYRS. I do so because those systems are not perfect and can always do more and do better for our city’s children. But I’ve dedicated my life to improving that system because I believe it truly can wrap around our children. DYRS, the Family Court, the delinquency attorneys, and the rich network of service providers who work tirelessly to lift up our youth and families are all immersed in a landscape that treats children as children. That network of systems, organizations, and individuals has collectively adopted a developmentally responsive lens to young people who have caused harm in our community. We are experienced in looking deeper than an act of violence to discern the unmet mental health needs or educational challenges and to untangle the untreated trauma.

I am so heartened to see so many individuals on today’s witness list who survived months or years of incarceration and are speaking up in an effort to spare others the harms they suffered. Although I wish that my client, Antoine, was here to share his own truth with you, I hope that sharing a bit about him will help this committee better understand the dire repercussions of charging youth like him in the adult system. When I met Antoine, he was 16 years old and on the cusp of leaving DYRS’s New Beginnings Youth Development Center. He moved home with his mom, reenrolled in school, and began taking driver’s education classes. But his family’s crushing poverty was also weighing on him and he turned to any means possible to help with family expenses. Two months later, Antoine was arrested for armed robbery and charged as an adult. He ultimately pleaded guilty to attempted robbery, an offense ineligible for direct file, but there was no way for him to move back into the juvenile system. Almost immediately upon being charged in the adult system, his newly appointed attorney reached out to me for help. Try as he might, the attorney was completely unable to convey to Antoine what was happening to him. Antoine, with his significant learning disabilities, could not understand why he was being charged and treated as an adult despite being only 17 years old. But off he went to federal prison, where he was unable to obtain any of his special education supports, was unable to pass the GED exam, and so was also unable to access any vocational services. He was stabbed, was unable to obtain proper medical care, was preyed upon by older inmates, and became suicidal. Upon his return to DC, Antoine fought debilitating depression, failed to reenroll in school, and struggled with CSOSA supervision. He then picked up his next charge – a misdemeanor for tampering with his GPS device, which ultimately landed him back at the DC Jail.

Charging Antoine as an adult launched him forward on a path that is all too familiar – the revolving door of the adult criminal legal system. Charging him as an adult didn’t make our community any safer. In fact, it did the exact opposite. With an adult criminal record and no high school diploma or GED, lawful employment was frustratingly elusive. He returned to criminal means to make ends meet. Had Antoine been able to remain in the juvenile legal system, he would have been eligible for DYRS supports until age 21. He could have accessed the mental health services, vocational training, and special education services he desperately needed to embark on a healthy adulthood. Antoine, and our community, is worse off today because of the decision of the US Attorney’s Office to charge him as an adult.

For Antoine and for all the Black and Brown children who have been charged as adults without careful consideration of their potential for rehabilitation, Open City Advocates strongly supports the Redefinition of the Child Amendment Act of 2021. Given that youth charged as adults are 34% more likely to commit future crimes than those charged in the juvenile system, our support for the Act is also for the safety of our entire community.¹

Thank you for this opportunity to testify and I am available for any questions.

### Written Testimony

Good morning, Chairman Allen and members of the Committee,

My name is Penelope Spain and I am the Chief Executive Officer and Co-Founder of Open City Advocates. Open City Advocates provides legal representation and holistic mentoring to youth who have been committed to the DC Department of Youth Rehabilitation Services (DYRS), including throughout their reentry from secure facilities. Our target population is teens 16 years or older, with a lengthy recidivism history who are most at-risk of entering the adult criminal legal system. If and when our clients find themselves in the adult system, we continue supporting them throughout their subsequent supervision, incarceration, and reentry whether they are returning home from the DC Jail or a federal prison.

Open City Advocates resolutely supports the Redefinition of the Child Amendment Act of 2021. During my 17 years serving young people with Open City Advocates, I have seen too many promising lives swept into the adult legal system through our jurisdiction’s direct file provisions. With no judicial oversight and seemingly arbitrary decisions by the US Attorney’s Office, youth who have already suffered so much trauma are tossed into a system that denies their humanity and further scars them, both physically and emotionally, at the very moment that they are trying to gain their footing as young adults. The DC Jail, federal prisons, and CSOSA supervision are set up as systems of punishment, sending a clear message to those in their grasp that they are hardened criminals with a debt to pay. But those who get ensnared through DC’s direct file laws are not intractable outlaws. They are children and it’s time to start treating them as children.

As you may remember, I have come before you and the DC Council many times over the past decade to push for improvements to DC’s juvenile legal system and to DYRS. I do so because those systems are not perfect and can always do more and do better for our city’s children. That network of systems, organizations, and individuals has collectively adopted a developmentally responsive lens to young people who have caused harm in our community. We are experienced in looking deeper than an act of violence to see the needs that lie beneath, to discern the unmet mental health or educational challenges, and to untangle the untreated trauma.

I worked with Antoine to explain how credit cards worked, that having an apartment required monthly payments not just a deposit, and that accessing unemployment benefits required more than working for one day. Despite his ambition, Antoine had so much to learn about the way the world worked! Two months later, Antoine was arrested for armed robbery and charged as an adult. But off he went to federal prison, where he was unable to obtain any of his special education supports, was unable to pass the GED exam, and so was also unable to access any vocational services.
