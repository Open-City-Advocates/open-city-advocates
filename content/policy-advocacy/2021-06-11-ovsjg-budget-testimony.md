**Budget Oversight Hearing**  
**Office of Victim Services and Justice Grants (OVSJG)**  
**Committee on the Judiciary and Public Safety, Council of the District of Columbia**  
**Ward 6 Councilmember Charles Allen, Chairperson**

**June 11, 2021**

## Testimony of Penelope Spain, Chief Executive Officer

Dear Chairman Allen and members of the committee,

My name is Penelope Spain and I am the Chief Executive Officer and Co-Founder of Open City Advocates, an OVSJG grantee. Open City Advocates provides legal representation and holistic mentoring to youth who have been committed to the DC Department of Youth Rehabilitation Services (DYRS), including throughout their reentry from secure facilities. If and when our clients find themselves in the adult criminal legal system, we continue supporting them throughout their subsequent supervision, incarceration, and reentry. Open City Advocates is also a member of the DC Reentry Action Network (RAN).

First and foremost, I would like to express our profound gratitude to the Office of Victim Services and Justice Grants (OVSJG) for their steadfast support of Open City Advocates. In 2006, only one year after Open City Advocates was established, OVSJG believed in our mission and funded us while we were still operating out of a closet in a warehouse in Ward 7. Fifteen years later and with the steadfast support of OVSJG, Open City Advocates has grown from grassroots legal advocacy and holistic mentoring to successfully convincing the DC Court of Appeals to recognize the right to legal counsel for all youth who are committed to DYRS. OVSJG's continuity of funding has allowed us to uplift the lives of youth returning from both juvenile and adult incarceration and to significantly reduce their rate of returning to incarceration.

Open City Advocates strongly supports the Mayor's proposed FY22 budget for OVSJG that includes $21 million for Justice Grants. This would include funding for both juvenile and adult reentry support. Like the clients we serve, these funds would support DC residents with the highest needs. Our clients struggle regularly with obtaining stable housing, vital documents, adequate mental health support, substance use/recovery support, and meaningful education and employment opportunities. This past year through our partnership with RAN, we were able to provide our recently returned clients with cell phones and basic service plans. Even such simple and cost-effective solutions make a huge difference for our clients' stability. When adequately funded, OVJSG can support a wide range of holistic and effective services. Robust funding for both juvenile and adult reentry support would allow OVSJG to support new and innovative solutions while also providing the critical continuity of support that transforms service providers like Open City Advocates into leading models of reentry support in the District of Columbia.

Robust funding for reentry services is also critical when we consider the deep racial disparities of the District's juvenile and criminal legal systems and the District's wide racial wealth divide. Open City Advocates meets our clients after they have been committed to DYRS, where 98% of committed youth are Black,¹ whereas only 52% of DC youth are Black.² These racial disparities continue into the adult system: while 46% of the DC population is Black,³ 86% of inmates at the DC jail are Black.⁴ We also know that the District leads the nation in income inequality and has one of the highest poverty rates in the country.⁵ The median income for Black residents is less than 1/3 of the median income for white residents, and Black families average 81 times less wealth than white families.⁶ As a result of these racial and economic disparities, our clients' families and friends are not in a position to support our clients to the extent needed when they return home from incarceration. The District must step in to fill the racial wealth gap so that all returning citizens have the support they need to successfully reintegrate back into the community. Robust investment in reentry services can help the District address its entrenched racial disparities, which will pay dividends in better public safety, economic, and social outcomes.

### Conclusion

On behalf of Open City Advocates, I would like to extend my gratitude to OVSJG for its continued support of and partnership in our work. Robust funding for juvenile and adult reentry services is exactly what the District needs right now. Thank you for your time and consideration.
