**Budget Oversight Hearing**

**Office of Victim Services and Justice Grants (OVSJG)**

**April 25, 2024**

**Committee on the Judiciary and Public Safety**

**Ward 2 Councilmember Brooke Pinto, Chairperson**

## Testimony of Whitney Louchheim, Chief Operating Officer

### Oral Testimony

Good morning, Councilmembers. My name is Whitney Louchheim. I am a resident of Ward 6 and for the past 19 years have served as the Co-Founder and Chief Operating Officer of Open City Advocates, which provides legal representation and holistic advocacy to youth who have been committed to DYRS.

I'd like to start today by expressing our gratitude to OVSJG for their steadfast support of Open City Advocates. In 2006, only one year after establishing our organization, OVSJG saw the importance of our mission and provided critical seed funding. At the time, Open City Advocates was still operating out of a closet, without heat or air conditioning, in a warehouse next to Marvin Gaye Park in Ward 7. Nineteen years later, we have assisted over 165 court-involved youth and have significantly cut the rate of recidivism for our clients. Through impact litigation, we also successfully established the right to legal counsel for all youth throughout their commitment to DYRS.

The mayor's proposed budget contains significant cuts to the programs that help to fund our work. In particular, cuts to the Access to Justice program would be devastating at a time when the District is experiencing a quickly growing population of committed youth.

Because of the support of OVSJG, Open City Advocates has been able to grow and deepen our practice and take on new clients. We have hired three staff attorneys with the support of OVSJG, and cuts to Access to Justice would mean that we cannot continue to employ attorneys to represent youth who are committed to DYRS. This would mean that some children committed to DYRS would go without the critical legal and holistic support that we provide.

Kids who are locked up need a lot of individualized support. They have questions about cell restrictions, how to access their school work, why they can't see their mom, or why they can't reach their trial attorney. A child might want to know why team meetings aren't taking place, or why they have not yet been assigned a Care Coordinator, or why they've been refused their "level" advancement three times in a row. A dedicated attorney like those at Open City Advocates can dig in, identify root causes, and begin to help address such issues.

When our clients are released, we strive to ensure their success in every aspect of their lives. We make sure that their needs are met, that they're enrolling in school, or finding a job. Even when our clients' commitment ends, we continue to support them. Our model of holistic advocacy means there's no break in our efforts to make sure they succeed.

Like other legal service providers, defense attorneys are an important part of the public safety landscape in the District. We are making sure **everyone** is safe: what we know from research–not just of our own data but nationwide–is that providing this holistic support really goes to the root of delinquency. By improving the lives of youth, we are helping to ensure that they don't go back to criminal behavior and that they don't become victims again either. Our clients–who we meet in the deepest end of the legal system–are much more likely to stay out of the system altogether.

Thank you. I am happy to answer any questions.

### Written Testimony

Good morning, Councilmembers. My name is Whitney Louchheim. I am a resident of Ward 6 and for the past 19 years have served as the Co-Founder and Chief Operating Officer of Open City Advocates, which provides legal representation and holistic advocacy to youth who have been committed to the DC Department of Youth Rehabilitation Services (DYRS).

I'd like to start today by expressing our profound gratitude to OVSJG for their steadfast support of Open City Advocates. In 2006, only one year after establishing our organization, OVSJG saw the importance of our mission and provided critical seed funding. At the time, Open City Advocates was still operating out of a closet, without heat or air conditioning, in a warehouse next to Marvin Gaye Park in Ward 7. Nineteen years later, we have assisted over 165 court-involved youth and have significantly cut the rate of recidivism for our clients. Through impact litigation, we also successfully established the right to legal counsel for all youth throughout their commitment to DYRS.

The mayor's proposed budget contains significant cuts to the programs that help to fund our work. In particular, cuts to the Access to Justice program would be devastating at a time when the District is experiencing a quickly growing population of committed youth.

It's impossible to untether the success of Open City Advocates and our funding from OVSJG because they have been a major supporter of our work from the beginning. We've been able to grow and deepen our practice and take on new clients because of the support of OVSJG. We have hired three staff attorneys with the support of both OVSJG and the Access to Justice program, and cuts to Access to Justice would mean that we cannot continue to employ attorneys to represent youth who are committed to DYRS. This would mean that some children committed to DYRS would go without the critical legal and holistic support that we provide.

Through the Public Defender Service of DC, the Federal government provides some legal services inside of DYRS, but they only fund three attorney positions who must represent as many as 158 children at a time. Kids who are locked up need a lot of individualized support. They have questions about cell restrictions, how to access their school work, why they can't see their mom, or why they can't reach their trial attorney. Those 158 young people need more support than can be provided by three Public Defender attorneys. For instance, a child might want to know why team decision-making meetings aren't taking place, or why they have not yet been assigned a Care Coordinator, or why they've been refused their "level" advancement three times in a row. A dedicated attorney like those at Open City Advocates can dig in, identify root causes, and begin to help address such issues.

The Public Defender can represent only a fraction of the population. Others are represented by Open City Advocates and by a panel of attorneys that we have helped the Family Court to certify and support (using federal funds) over the last two years. But as the number of committed youth increases, the need for this support increases as well. In fact, we are still trying to catch up with the increases in commitment as children go unrepresented today.

Open City Advocates supports our clients holistically: we make sure that their basic needs are met, that they're enrolling in and attending school, getting transportation, or finding a job or vocational training. Even when our clients' commitment ends, we continue to support them. They are still at risk of being disconnected from school and from work, and we keep working. Our model of holistic advocacy means there's no break in our efforts to make sure they succeed.

Like other civil legal service providers, defense attorneys are an important part of the public safety institutions in the District. We are making sure **everyone** is safe: what we know from research–not just of our own data but nationwide–is that providing this holistic support really goes to the root of delinquency. By improving the lives of youth, we are helping to ensure that they don't go back to criminal behavior and that they don't become victims again either. Our clients–who we meet in the deepest end of the legal system–are much more likely to stay out of the system altogether.

Over the past few years, we've been thrilled to see the difference in our clients' lives that funds through the American Rescue Plan Act have made, and we think these are lessons for the future and for budget priorities in particular.

By providing living-wage stipends to our clients attending school and workforce development programs, and by providing ancillary support for things like phones, transportation, clothing, and food, we have helped to get our clients out of the cycles of poverty that got them involved in the juvenile legal system in the first place. This makes our whole community safer. ARPA Flex Funds have been immensely valuable to our clients' success and to their ability to escape the cycles of poverty and violence. These funds have been so valuable that, in the coming year, we are going to do everything we can to replace those funds through either public or private sources: wherever we can find the resources to assist our clients.

Thank you. I am happy to answer any questions.

## Testimony of Ramon Bunch, Board Member

Hello, my name is Ramon Bunch. I am a Ward 6 resident and a former client of Open City Advocates. Today I work as a user interface and experience designer at a major defense contractor and serve as a member of the Board of Directors for Open City Advocates.

I knew Whitney and her co-founder Penelope when they were law students at American University, even before they started operating out of that warehouse closet next to Marvin Gaye Park without any heat or air conditioning. Meeting them radically transformed my life: I used to want to be the biggest drug dealer in DC; now I'm aiming to be the best UI/UX designer. That's because of Whitney and Penelope.

The facility I spent time in was called Oak Hill. It was incredibly unsafe, I was attacked a few times at first. Frequently the whole place had staffing ratios of 30 to 1. (The standard is 8 to 1, maybe 12 to 1 while kids are sleeping). I would be put in the hole routinely, sometimes for safety reasons, sometimes because the officers were just brutal. Open City Advocates was part of the coalition that got Oak Hill shut down, and I know their work with me as a client played a role there.

Shortly after I got out, I was stabbed because I was living in a neighborhood that wasn't very safe. Penelope and Whitney put me up in a hotel in Northwest and I got a bunch of different jobs: a grocery store, a bookstore, a few other things. It kept me busy: I had some responsibilities that really got my mindset out of the street lifestyle, I started making friends who weren't connected to that life, and I realized that I'd been missing this whole different world to what I was used to.

Because of OCA's work, I realized you can actually live without having to sell drugs and have fights over stupid turf, that you can walk around without a weapon and still be safe. I moved into an apartment and ended up going to college for a while for sign language interpretation, but the work I do now pays better and doesn't require a degree. OCA encouraged me to try different things!

Another cool thing that OCA has done recently through OVSJG funding is to provide a stipend to youth attending school or skills training, as well as other necessities like transportation or a phone. This has been so successful in helping our clients escape the cycles of poverty and violence–cycles that I had been in–that OCA is going to do everything we can to replace those funds.

Thank you for your time. I'm happy to answer any questions.
