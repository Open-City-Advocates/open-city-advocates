**Public Hearing on B23-0291, the "Detained Youth Access to Juvenile Services Program Amendment Act of 2019"**  
**Committee on Recreation and Youth Affairs, Council of the District of Columbia**  
**Councilmember Trayon White**

**October 17, 2019**

## Testimony of Penelope Spain, Co-Founder & Chief Executive Officer

Good morning, Councilmember White and the Committee on Recreation and Youth Affairs. My name is Penelope Spain and I am a court-appointed CJA defense attorney for youth in DC's juvenile justice system. I am also the Co-Founder and CEO of Open City Advocates, a nonprofit organization that provides legal representation and holistic mentoring to youth who have been committed to the DC Department of Youth Rehabilitation Services. Today, as a representative of Open City Advocates and also in my capacity as a CJA delinquency attorney, I express my strong support for the "Detained Youth Access to Juvenile Services Program Amendment Act of 2019."

The Public Defender Service's Juvenile Services Program (JSP) is imperative to my ability to provide zealous legal representation to my clients. First and foremost, JSP's staff within the Youth Services Center (YSC) and New Beginnings facilitates the most fundamental aspect of my legal counsel – confidential communication with my clients. When one of my clients is in YSC or New Beginnings, it is always a stressful and traumatic experience. Knowing that their attorney is aware of their situation and is doing everything possible on their behalf helps my clients know that they are not forgotten while locked up. As such, whenever I learn of a client's arrest, one of the first things I do is reach out to the JSP team asking them to either facilitate a confidential call between me and my client or, if that is not possible, to relay an update to my client. Something as basic as a phone call with their lawyer can help a young person remain calm, which, in a locked facility, can make all the difference. When young people feel frustrated and feel like no one is looking out for them, they tend to give up hope or lash out at others, sometimes resulting in additional criminal charges for assaults on other residents or staff. Regular communication with their trusted attorney can help prevent that.

In addition to facilitating timely communication with attorneys, JSP is the only non-DYRS entity that has regular access to youth within the facilities and that can call attention to shortcomings in services or conditions of confinement. For example, I once had a client who was severely depressed and suicidal. A few days had passed since I had last spoken with my client and I was unaware that he was isolating himself in his room and refusing schooling and all other services. It was a JSP attorney who alerted me to the situation, which prompted me to visit with the client in person the very next day. Without JSP bringing the situation to my attention, the young man may have refused services for additional days or weeks. In addition to the negative mental health effects, this also could have prolonged his confinement. At the meeting with my client, I was able to address his medication needs and provide him with the encouragement he needed to push forward with services and move closer to release.

As an attorney with over 14 years of experience representing youth under the supervision and custody of the DC Department of Youth Rehabilitation Services, I can attest that without JSP my legal representation would suffer greatly. JSP is integral to my ability to communicate with my clients who are being held at YSC or New Beginnings, to obtain timely information from my clients, and to troubleshoot shortcomings in my client's care. JSP not only helps me do my job, but to do it better. In the end, that means that the children in our nation's capital receive the representation they deserve. I urge you to move this legislation forward as quickly as possible to codify JSP's presence within DYRS's secure facilities and to ensure that the voices of our young people are heard and that their legal rights are protected.

Thank you for giving me this opportunity to testify. I welcome any questions you or any other Councilmembers may have.
