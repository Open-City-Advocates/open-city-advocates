**Performance Oversight Hearing: Access to Justice**  
**Committee on the Judiciary and Public Safety, Council of the District of Columbia**

**March 2, 2026**

## Testimony of Penelope Spain, Chief Executive Officer

Good morning, Councilmembers. My name is Penelope Spain. I am a resident of Ward 4 and for over 21 years have served as Co-Founder and CEO of Open City Advocates, which provides legal representation and holistic advocacy to youth committed to the Department of Youth Rehabilitation Services or DYRS.

The funding and support provided through OVSJG, particularly through the Access to Justice program, doesn’t just support my organization and my team. It supports DC’s most vulnerable children and strengthens our entire community.

Let me share a few examples. Earlier this year, a young teenager in our program was placed in a facility hundreds of miles from here. When our attorney visited, she learned the child had been abused by staff at the facility. No one from DYRS was regularly checking in on this child’s wellbeing. No family member had been able to visit. It was his Open City Advocates attorney (funded through Access to Justice) who uncovered what was happening, who immediately sounded alarms about the placement, and who successfully fought for him to be returned to his community.

Without Access to Justice funding, that child would not have had a post-commitment attorney. He likely would have remained what we call “out of state, out of mind,” suffering ongoing abuse, and only returning to the District months later with more trauma to process than when he’d left.

Twenty years ago, OVSJG provided critical seed funding to Open City Advocates when we were still operating out of a closet in a warehouse in Ward 7. Since then, support from OVSJG has allowed us to establish, through impact litigation, the right to legal counsel for *every* young person committed to DYRS. Although that right now exists on paper, it only becomes real when our city funds attorneys to do the work.

And the need for post-commitment attorneys exists not only when we send children to far away placements, but also when we opt to treat our children in our city’s own facilities, including the Youth Services Center and New Beginnings. When one of our clients recently spiraled downwards and was receiving no meaningful treatment from DYRS, one of our attorneys fought for judicial review. A new plan was developed and the child was placed at home with services. Later, when family dynamics deteriorated and the child’s mother pleaded for help, it was our attorney who stepped in and provided critical family mediation and linkage to ongoing family support services. You see, a dedicated post-commitment attorney doesn’t just fight in court or administrative hearings. They familiarize themselves with the unmet needs of the client, the family, and even the community. And then strive to fill those gaps. In order to meet the “legal” needs of our clients, to help them turn away from criminal tendencies, they also have to address the unmet educational, financial, housing, mental health needs, and so much more. And by so doing, they help stabilize young people, families, and entire communities.

None of that happens without dedicated counsel. OVSJG funding, through Access to Justice, makes it possible for us to employ and support the attorneys who do this work every day. We ask the Council to sustain that investment.

Thank you. I am happy to answer any questions.

## Testimony of Joshua Miller, PhD, Research & Advocacy Director

Good morning, Councilmembers. My name is Joshua Miller. I am the Research and Advocacy Director at Open City Advocates. I want to tell you specifically what OVSJG's Justice Grants funding made possible this past year.

With OVSJG support, we served 66 clients in the last grant year. Youth locked up at New Beginnings and the Youth Services Center, young adults returning to their communities, and formerly committed individuals now in the adult system. In the last quarter alone, five clients obtained jobs, three of them for the first time. Three clients re-enrolled in school, each citing the stipend we provide as the reason they went back. Six families were reunified. Seven clients met legal goals, including record sealing and case dismissals.

Let me be concrete about what this advocacy looks like. Our CEO already mentioned two cases involving civil issues that are supported by Access to Justice funding. But OVSJG also provides critical support for matters that more directly touch the juvenile and adult criminal justice system. In two other cases involving cross-jurisdicational issues in Maryland, we partnered with Maryland defenders and provided detailed, positive information about our clients. As a result, one had his case transferred from the adult to the juvenile system, and the other had his case dismissed altogether. In two more cases, our attorneys mediated family disputes and got both young people back home with their families.

OVSJG funding also allows us to intervene in cases where our clients face federal prosecution. During the last fiscal year, one of our clients was facing up to 16 years in a federal case. Our attorney helped the court understand that commitment to DYRS is not the same as continuous incarceration. This simple explanation of DC’s juvenile justice system resulted in a 73 percent reduction of the federal sentencing guidelines. The judge ultimately sentenced our client to three years, sparing him decades of trauma in the adult federal prison system. Another client was facing over a year of reincarceration at a parole revocation hearing. Our attorney, who had mentored him for years, testified about his accomplishments and character, and he received 28 days instead. In each case, it was an Open City Advocates attorney, funded by OVSJG Justice Grants, who made the difference.

These are not things that happen on their own. DYRS is experiencing significant delays in case planning, placement, and reentry preparation. These delays make our work harder and make it more necessary than ever. When a young person sits at the Youth Services Center for months without a placement, it is our attorneys who are pushing for answers. When parents cannot visit their children in the hospital because DYRS transported them there, it is Open City Advocates raising that issue before the Family Court's Juvenile Subcommittee.

This is what OVSJG's Justice Grants produce: attorneys who understand the full breadth of their clients’ needs, and who stay in their lives for years. Continued investment means continued results.

Thank you. I am happy to answer any questions.
