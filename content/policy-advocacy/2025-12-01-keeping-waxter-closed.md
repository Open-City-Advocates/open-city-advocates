The Waxter proposal was DYRS’s answer to crowding at DC’s Youth Services Center (YSC), the city’s youth jail. YSC holds children waiting for their cases to be decided and committed children waiting for DYRS to place them. It was built for 98 children, and in fiscal year 2025 it was over capacity on 344 days.

In October 2025, DYRS Director Sam Abed told the DC Council that the agency wanted to acquire or lease a facility in Maryland, near New Beginnings, DYRS’s secure facility in Laurel. The facility he described matched the Thomas J.S. Waxter Children’s Center, a Maryland youth prison closed in 2022 after years of documented problems. Abed had overseen that closure as head of Maryland’s Department of Juvenile Services.

Maryland’s Juvenile Justice Monitoring Unit had reported on Waxter for years: extreme temperatures, mold, sewage smells, and crumbling buildings, and repeated findings that it was unfit to hold children. We turned those reports into a public statement on November 6, shared it with journalists and officials, and brought the findings to the Council in [our November 13 testimony](/policy-advocacy/2025-11-13-strengthening-capacity-testimony).

Advocates in DC and Maryland fought the proposal together. DC Justice Lab, Advance Maryland, the ACLU of DC, and the ACLU of Maryland coordinated the opposition, and the two ACLU affiliates issued a joint statement against reopening Waxter. Anne Arundel County Council President Julie Hummer opposed returning Waxter to detention. On December 1, The Washington Post reported that Maryland had told Abed in August that Waxter was unavailable. After weeks of public pressure, the proposal was dropped, and Waxter stayed closed.

Keeping Waxter closed was a victory, and the children at YSC still need an answer. What would relieve the crowding is moving children out faster and keeping more of them from going in: respite and shelter beds for young people who need a safe place to sleep, quicker placements so committed youth aren’t waiting in a jail, and mental health care, housing, and family support at home.
