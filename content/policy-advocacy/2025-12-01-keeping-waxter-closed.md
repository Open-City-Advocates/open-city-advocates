The Waxter proposal was DYRS’s answer to crowding at DC’s Youth Services Center (YSC), the city’s youth jail. YSC holds children waiting for their cases to be decided and committed children waiting for the Department of Youth Rehabilitation Services (DYRS) to place them. It was built for 98 children, and in fiscal year 2025 it was over capacity on 344 days.

In October 2025, DYRS Director Sam Abed proposed acquiring or leasing another facility. The facility he described matched Waxter, a Maryland youth prison closed in 2022 after years of documented problems. Abed had overseen that closure as head of Maryland’s Department of Juvenile Services.

When an agency locks up children, it must answer for what happens to them. Open City Advocates brought Waxter’s conditions into public view: we turned the state monitors’ reports into a public statement, shared them with journalists and officials, and brought the findings into [Council testimony](/policy-advocacy/2025-11-13-strengthening-capacity-testimony).

Advocates in DC and Maryland fought the proposal together. DC Justice Lab, Advance Maryland, the ACLU of DC, and the ACLU of Maryland coordinated the opposition. Anne Arundel County Council President Julie Hummer opposed returning Waxter to detention. On December 1, [The Washington Post reported](https://www.washingtonpost.com/dc-md-va/2025/11/29/dc-juvenile-detention-sam-abed/) that Maryland had told Abed in August that Waxter was unavailable. After weeks of public pressure, the proposal was dropped, and Waxter stayed closed. Proposals like this rarely end on a single day, so we kept asking: in [our February 2026 oversight testimony](/policy-advocacy/2026-02-02-dyrs-performance-oversight-testimony), we asked Director Abed for the status of the negotiations.

Keeping Waxter closed was a victory. The children at YSC still need an answer. Children belong in their communities. DC should fund respite and shelter beds for young people who need a safe place to sleep, move committed youth out of jail faster, and invest in mental health care, housing, and family support so more young people can stay home.

*Sources: DYRS YSC fact sheet and oversight testimony; Maryland Juvenile Justice Monitoring Unit, 2018 Annual Review; Washington Post, Dec. 1, 2025.*
