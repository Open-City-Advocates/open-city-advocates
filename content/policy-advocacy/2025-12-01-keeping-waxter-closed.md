In October 2025, the Department of Youth Rehabilitation Services (DYRS) proposed acquiring or leasing another detention facility in Maryland to address overcrowding at DC’s Youth Services Center (YSC). Director Sam Abed’s description pointed to the Thomas J.S. Waxter Children’s Center, near DYRS’s New Beginnings facility in Laurel. Maryland had closed Waxter in 2022 after years of documented problems. Abed had overseen that closure as head of Maryland’s Department of Juvenile Services.

YSC, the city’s youth jail, holds children waiting for their cases to be decided and committed children waiting for DYRS to place them. It was built for 98 children. In its February 2026 performance oversight responses, DYRS reported that YSC had been over capacity on 344 days of fiscal year 2025.

Maryland’s Juvenile Justice Monitoring Unit had documented extreme temperatures, mold, sewage smells, and deteriorating buildings at Waxter, and had repeatedly called for its closure. Its [2018 annual review](https://msa.maryland.gov/megafile/msa/speccol/sc5300/sc5339/000113/024300/024370/20200165e.pdf#page=25) included photographs of the conditions girls were living in.

We turned those reports into a public statement on November 6, shared it with journalists and officials, and brought the findings to the Council in [our November 13 testimony](/policy-advocacy/2025-11-13-strengthening-capacity-testimony). Independent monitors had gathered the evidence that let us challenge the proposal. We also urged the Council to restore independent monitoring in DC, where funding for the Office of Independent Juvenile Justice Facilities Oversight had lapsed on October 1.

We worked with DC Justice Lab, Advance Maryland, the ACLU of DC, and the ACLU of Maryland to oppose reopening Waxter. The two ACLU affiliates issued a [joint statement](https://www.acludc.org/press-releases/aclu-d-c-and-aclu-md-statement-on-d-c-agency-re-opening-a-condemned-juvenile-facility-in-maryland/) against the proposal. Anne Arundel County Council President Julie Hummer also opposed returning Waxter to detention.

On December 1, The Washington Post reported that Maryland had told Abed in August that Waxter was unavailable. We made Waxter’s documented conditions part of the public debate and pressed DYRS to explain why it was pursuing the property. Waxter remained closed. Questions about DYRS’s plans continued: in [our February 2026 oversight testimony](/policy-advocacy/2026-02-02-dyrs-performance-oversight-testimony), we asked about the status of negotiations.

Children belong in their communities. The District should invest in the housing, mental health care, family support, and community-based programs that help them stay there. Young people who need a safe place to sleep need respite and shelter beds. Committed children should receive appropriate placements promptly, instead of waiting for months in a jail.
