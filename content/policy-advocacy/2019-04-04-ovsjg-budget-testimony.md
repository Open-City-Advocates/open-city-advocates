**Budget Hearing for the Office of Victim Services and Justice Grants**  
**Committee on the Judiciary and Public Safety, Council of the District of Columbia**  
**Chairman Charles Allen**

**April 4, 2019**

## Testimony of Penelope Spain, Chief Executive Officer and Co-Founder

Dear Chairman Allen and members of the committee,

My name is Penelope Spain and I am the Chief Executive Officer and Co-Founder of Open City Advocates. Open City Advocates provides legal representation and mentoring services to youth residing at New Beginnings and throughout their reentry. If and when our clients find their way into the adult system, we continue supporting them throughout their subsequent supervision, incarceration, and reentry. Open City Advocates is also a member of the DC Reentry Action Network (RAN). I want to begin by thanking Mayor Bowser for the $500,000 she added to the Office of Victim Services and Justice Grants' proposed FY20 budget for community-based reentry services. While Open City Advocates is very thankful for the increased funding for OVSJG, the needs of the District's returning citizens are many, and this does not yet come close to meeting them. Therefore, we join RAN in asking that an additional $800,000 in local dollars be allocated to OVSJG for community-based reentry services, for a total of $2.74 million in local dollar funding. This increase will allow organizations like Open City Advocates to continue supporting recently returned citizens and their communities.

For the past 14 years, and with the support of OVSJG funding, we have helped many young people in DC through the reentry process, whether they are returning from the Bureau of Prisons (BOP) or the DC jail. Their needs are sometimes basic but often voluminous, including metro fare, food, housing, furniture, and clothing. Stable employment and housing remain the greatest hurdles, which were recently exacerbated by the closure of DC's last independent living program for youth committed to the DC Department of Youth Rehabilitation Services, Life Deeds. Open City Advocates dedicates staff time to identifying resources for clients on everything from job opportunities to mental health services to applying for social security disability benefits. We help clients get DC identification cards and set up bank accounts. We help them re-enroll in school so that they can obtain a high school diploma, GED, or pursue higher education. We also actively partner with other RAN organizations to provide our clients with additional support services and resources and to work on systemic improvements to the juvenile and criminal justice systems.

Robust funding for reentry services is also critical when we consider the deep racial disparities of the District's juvenile and criminal justice systems and the District's deep racial wealth gap. We meet our clients after they have been committed to the Department of Youth Rehabilitation Services, and 97% of committed youth are black,¹ whereas only 54% of DC youth are black.² These racial disparities continue into the adult system: while about 47% of the DC population at large is black,³ 90% of male inmates and 86% of female inmates at the jail are black.⁴ We also know that the District leads the nation in income inequality and has one of the highest poverty rates in the country.⁵ The median income for black residents is less than 1/3 of the median income for white residents.⁶ The poverty rate of black residents is nearly four times higher than that of white residents.⁷ Black families average 81 times less wealth than white families.⁸ As a result of these racial and economic disparities, our clients' families and friends are often not in a position to support them to the extent needed when they return home from incarceration. For example, one of our clients recently came home from the BOP and a family member offered him a place to live, but it was entirely unfurnished, without even a bed to sleep on. We located donated household items and used our own funds to deliver them to his apartment. The District must step in to fill the racial wealth gap so that all returning citizens have the support they need to successfully reintegrate back into the community. Robust investment in reentry services can help the District address its entrenched racial disparities, which will pay dividends in better public safety, economic, and social outcomes.

### Conclusion

On behalf of Open City Advocates, I would like to extend my gratitude to the OVSJG for its continued support of and partnership in our work. Additional funding for reentry services will allow RAN member organizations like ours to better serve our returning community members. Thank you for your time and consideration.
