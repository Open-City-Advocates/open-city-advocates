**Public Hearing on B23-0039, the "Special Education Rights for Youth Defendants Amendment Act of 2019"**  
**Committee on the Judiciary and Public Safety, Council of the District of Columbia**  
**Chairman Charles Allen**

**July 11, 2019**

## Testimony of Penelope Spain, Co-Founder & Chief Executive Officer

Good morning Chairman Allen and members of the Judiciary Committee. My name is Penelope Spain and I am a resident of Ward 6. I am a court-appointed CJA delinquency attorney for youth in DC's Family Court, as well as the Co-Founder and CEO of Open City Advocates. Open City Advocates is a nonprofit organization that provides legal representation and holistic mentoring services to youth who have been committed to the DC Department of Youth Rehabilitation Services and sent to New Beginnings Youth Development Center. We support our clients throughout their reentry and often even after their commitment to DYRS has ended. If and when our clients find their way into the adult criminal justice system, we continue supporting them throughout their subsequent supervision, incarceration, and reentry. Today, as a representative of Open City Advocates and also in my capacity as a CJA attorney, I express my support for the "Special Education Rights for Youth Defendants Amendment Act of 2019" and the edits to the bill as suggested by the School Justice Project.

Soon after establishing Open City Advocates in 2005 and in my earliest days serving as a delinquency attorney in DC Family Court, I saw quite clearly the positive impact on the outcomes for my clients when they had a qualified and engaged education attorney protecting and pursuing their educational rights. Beginning in 2014, my organization began cross-referring clients with the School Justice Project, which as you are aware, provides special education legal services to students with disabilities ages 17-22 who are involved in DC's juvenile or criminal justice systems. Prior to our partnership with the School Justice Project, I made numerous attempts to educate myself in the intricacies of special education law but will confess that I found it incredibly complex and, quite frankly, overwhelming. When my delinquency clients had the need for special education legal services, it became abundantly clear that they were best off when I or the Family Court could link them with another attorney who was specialized in that practice.

Over the years, a fair number of my clients have, unfortunately, graduated into the adult criminal justice system. Although those cases pain me greatly, they are balanced by the high school graduations, GED and vocational certification celebrations that I have attended. At such ceremonies, I often have had a special education attorney sitting next to me cheering on our shared client. That is not to say, however, that a stay at the DC Jail or a prison sentence served in the Federal Bureau of Prisons forecloses such education opportunities. With the right supports during their incarceration and throughout reentry, young people caught up in the criminal justice system can achieve their education goals. This bill takes a crucial first step in ensuring that our young people, be they behind bars or in the community, have the legal representation they need to graduate from high school, pursue higher education, and begin pulling themselves out of the poverty, trauma, and mental health needs that have held them back for most of their lives.

Through this bill, I hope that this Committee and the DC Council will recognize the promise in all our city's young people, be they thriving in the community or struggling to overcome the challenges of our juvenile and criminal justice systems. They are young people regardless of the system they are in. I firmly believe that we should provide the young people in our criminal justice system with the same supports and opportunities provided to those who are often just a few months younger and in our juvenile justice system, particularly when those supports are key to them pursuing their education. Providing youth in the adult criminal justice system with access to special education attorneys throughout their cases and sentences will improve their life outcomes. Such services will also increase community safety as our young people will be able to better rely on their education and employment opportunities to financially provide for themselves and their families instead of resorting to criminal means.

Thank you for giving me this opportunity to testify. I welcome any questions you or any other Councilmembers may have.
