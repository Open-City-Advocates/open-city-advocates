**Public Hearing on B23-0921, the Education and Credit Continuity Amendment Act of 2020**  
**Committee of the Whole and Committee on Education, Council of the District of Columbia**  
**Chairperson Phil Mendelson**

**November 24, 2020**

## Testimony of Penelope Spain, Co-Founder and CEO

Good morning, Chairperson Mendelson, Councilmembers Grosso and Pinto, and the Committee on Education. My name is Penelope Spain and I am the Co-Founder and CEO of Open City Advocates. Our organization provides legal representation and holistic mentoring to youth who have been committed to the DC Department of Youth Rehabilitation Services. We begin working with our clients after they have been removed from their homes and placed in a secure facility and we continue supporting them through the reentry process. Today, I express Open City Advocates' strong support for the Education Credit Continuity Amendment Act of 2020.

Over the last 15 years, we have seen firsthand the importance and value of education for system-involved youth. Access to quality education promotes numerous positive outcomes for our clients. Education increases employment opportunities, decreases the risk of recidivism, and helps reconnect them to their community.

Importantly, graduation with a high school diploma also has a profound impact on young people's self-confidence. Recently, one of our clients completed his GED after years of disillusionment with the school system and multiple DYRS placement disruptions. Since earning his GED, this client has gained the confidence to work three jobs and has developed a keen desire to pursue higher education.

Youth committed to DYRS experience multiple placements and transitions during their supervision. Each placement typically means another school disruption, and youth frequently lose credits with each transition. When young people in temporary placements believe the time spent in school will not count toward graduation requirements, they are less likely to genuinely engage in the coursework. Many of our clients have felt disheartened by the lack of credit accrual, and are therefore, less likely to reenroll and attend school upon their release.¹

When our clients return to the community, they face significant educational barriers. They often struggle to register for school and without careful oversight are often enrolled in inappropriate courses that do not help them meet graduation requirements. The hours they spent in school while in secure facilities do not always transfer to their new school. Our clients are routinely told they must repeat classes because the credits cannot transfer. These students, their families, and their advocates spend countless hours navigating bureaucratic hurdles just to access transcripts, school records, and have IEPs updated. For too many of our clients, these barriers completely extinguish their desire to pursue an education.

The Education Credit Continuity Amendment Act addresses many of the barriers system-involved youth face. The bill requires a streamlined interagency process for obtaining records from each placement. With the formalized point-of-contact, youth will no longer have to navigate complex bureaucracies just to enroll in school. A dedicated transition team will ease the reentry process and ensure that youth have an educational plan in place before they leave a placement. Lastly, the partial credit requirement guarantees that students will receive credit for the work they do and decreases the chances that they will feel discouraged about completing their education.

Open City Advocates strongly supports this legislation and believes that it is a significant step toward improving the lives of system-involved youth. Thank you for this opportunity to testify. I welcome any questions you or any other Councilmembers may have.
