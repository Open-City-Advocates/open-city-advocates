**OVSJG Performance Oversight Hearing**  
**Judiciary and Public Safety Committee, Council of the District of Columbia**

**January 29, 2025**

## Testimony of Penelope Spain, Co-Founder & Chief Executive Officer

Good afternoon, Councilmembers. My name is Penelope Spain. I am a resident of Ward 4 and the CEO and Co-Founder of Open City Advocates, a nonprofit that has served the District for over two decades by providing legal representation and holistic advocacy to youth committed to the Department of Youth Rehabilitation Services (DYRS). Our work includes significant support for our clients throughout their DYRS commitment and during their reentry from secure facilities. In the unfortunate situations where our clients enter the adult criminal legal system, we continue supporting them throughout their subsequent supervision, incarceration, and reentry.

Open City Advocates has been fortunate to collaborate closely with the Office of Victim Services and Justice Grants (OVSJG) since 2006 and more recently with the DC Bar Foundation’s Access to Justice program. These partnerships have been instrumental in funding and expanding our critical work, not only to provide robust, holistic client services, but also to tackle systemic issues that perpetuate cycles of delinquency and poverty.

### The Need for Our Services

Open City Advocates’ clients have experienced the inequity and systemic racism that pervade our society on a near-daily level since their earliest years. By working with youth committed to DYRS, we tackle racial disparity where it is most extreme.

As highlighted by recent data, 96% of youth committed to DYRS are Black, with the remaining 4% being Latino. (See https://dyrs.dc.gov/page/data-1.) Our young people face extreme disparities from the moment they enter the system. For instance:

- They are more likely to be *stopped* by police (10 times the rate of white children),
- 99% of children *searched* by police are Black,
- And 95% of all “use of force” incidents involve Black youth.(See https://dckidscount.org/health-safety/)

These statistics underscore the systemic challenges our clients face, which necessitate our holistic approach. Our clients have frequently experienced significant trauma, including at the hands of the legal system, and therefore it is imperative to build trust and provide comprehensive support.

At Open City Advocates, we have found that our clients cannot build trust with DYRS, comply with supervision, and fully engage in meaningful services without *first* having an opportunity to share their lived truth with a trusted ally who is able to fully acknowledge those traumatic experiences and their deep, long-lasting effects. From the moment Open City Advocates receives a client referral, our staff embarks on a healing process with each child. Through mentoring, getting to know their family, and understanding the unmet needs that propelled them into the juvenile system, we help our clients heal. We support them in finding their voice and cultivating their dreams. From that point of strength and with the aid of a trusted advocate, our clients can more genuinely engage with DYRS and service providers to move towards healing and the achievement of their goals.

### Impact and Achievements

Like other civil legal service providers, Open City Advocate’s post-sentencing defense attorneys are an important part of the public safety institutions in the District. We are making sure everyone is safe: what we know from research—not just of our own data but nationwide—is that providing this holistic support really goes to the root of delinquency. By improving the lives of youth, we are helping to ensure that they don't go back to delinquent or criminal behavior or cause further harm to individuals and our broader community. By helping our young people heal, we are far better able to weave them back into the fabric of our community, making children, families, and our neighborhoods safer and stronger.

With support from OVSJG, Open City Advocates is also able to provide living-wage stipends to our clients who are attending school and workforce development programs. We are also able to provide ancillary support for necessities like phones, transportation, clothing, and food. These supports are critical in helping our clients escape the cycles of poverty that so often got them involved in the juvenile legal system in the first place. With Open City Advocates’ active representation and holistic support, 81% of our clients have continued their education and 65% found employment. Again, this makes our whole community safer and stronger.

In truth, it feels nearly impossible to untether the success of Open City Advocates from the funding and profound programmatic support we have received from OVSJG since the earliest days of launching our program. In addition to improving the individual lives of our clients, their families, and our community, OVSJG has also been instrumental in our systemic reform efforts to improve the lives of all court-involved children in our city. For example, in 2020 through impact litigation, Open City Advocates successfully established the right to legal counsel for all youth throughout their commitment to DYRS. OVSJG enthusiastically supported our subsequent collaboration with DC Family Court to establish the country’s first panel of specialized, court-appointed attorneys to represent youth committed to DYRS.

In closing, Open City Advocates is committed to supporting court-involved youth and their families through our mission of equity and justice. We urge continued support for OVSJG and the Access to Justice program to sustain our work. By addressing systemic inequities and providing our clients with the resources and support they need, we can create a more equitable future for young people in the District of Columbia.

We are grateful for your work keeping this critical work funded in tough budget years. Thank you for your attention, and I welcome any questions you may have.
